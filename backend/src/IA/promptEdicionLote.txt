DO NOT DRAW THIS PLOT TOO SMALL.

This is the failure to avoid, and it has happened in every previous attempt. Measured
against the parking stalls in the result, the plot came back reading as 0.4 hectares when
it is {HECTAREAS}. Everything was drawn two to five times too large for the land it sits on.

A parking stall is 2.6 m wide — that size is fixed by regulation and is how the result will
be checked. Across the {FRENTE} m short side of this plot, {CAJONES} stalls fit side by
side. If fewer than that would fit across your drawing, everything in it is too big: make
every object smaller and put more of them in.

{CONCEPTO}

Develop the whole length of the strip, not just the end near the road. Place 40 or more
distinct elements, organised as a sequence of programme areas linked by a circulation spine
with secondary paths. Weave planted vegetation and green pockets between the built areas.
Images 2 and onward are reference photos of built developments: match how densely packed

THE PARKING LOT IS THE ONE THING THAT COMES OUT WRONG
Measured against the plot, parking has consistently been drawn at twice its correct size:
stalls 4.9 m wide instead of 2.5 m. Everything else in the scene comes out right, so build
the parking to the same scale as the rest.

A stall is 2.5 m wide by 5 m deep. A double-loaded aisle — two rows of stalls facing a
6 m drive lane — is 16 m across in total. Across the {FRENTE} m width of this plot, six
such aisles would fit side by side. A 60-car lot occupies roughly 35 x 35 m, which is
about one third of the plot's width and a twentieth of its length. If your parking looks
bigger than that, halve it.

RULES
1. Build ONLY inside the outlined strip. Everything outside stays pixel-for-pixel identical:
   the same road, the same forest, the same neighbouring plots, the same shadows.
2. Remove the white outline from the output. It is a guide, not part of the scene.
3. Keep the exact camera: same altitude, same top-down angle, same framing.
4. Keep the same light: same sun direction, same shadow length, same colour temperature.
5. Match the photographic style of image 1: same grain, same saturation, same realism.
   It must look like the same drone photo taken after the project was built.
6. No text, no labels, no numbers, no arrows, no legends, no watermarks, no borders.

OUTPUT
A single photorealistic aerial image, indistinguishable in style from image 1, with the plot
developed along its whole length, the band at its original thickness, and everything around
it unchanged.
